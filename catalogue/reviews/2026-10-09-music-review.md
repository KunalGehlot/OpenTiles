## Method

- Hand-written tunes (54 files incl. - Converted pieces (27): downloaded the Mutopia piece page, MIDI and LilyPond file, rebuilt the note list from the MIDI and compared it with arrangement.txt by onset and pitch (every piece: 0 missing, 0 extra), then checked licence on the page (all 'Public Domain'), partial/time/key against the LilyPond file, dropped ornaments, held notes cut, chords wider than an octave, hand crossing, how the piece ends, and read the printed PDF for the doubtful ones.
- Difficulty: compared with the song-model estimator (all within one level) and the scale in REVIEWING.md.
- Scripts live only in the scratchpad; nothing outside catalogue/ was changed; nothing was staged or committed; verified flags untouched.

## General notes for all converted pieces

- The converter keeps every onset (nothing lost except ornaments), but it cuts held notes at the next onset in the same hand, so inner or held notes look short (Consolation, Morning Prayer, The Entertainer).
- It drops notes that are not on a sixteenth grid and shorter than a sixteenth: grace notes (harmless) but also real thirty-seconds and sextuplets. Real losses: Invention 14 (136 notes), Für Elise complete (67), Beethoven WoO 82 (8). Everywhere else at most 9 notes.
- Repeats are not played in the MIDI of most pieces, so each repeated section sounds once; that is acceptable, except where a D.C. is needed to reach the real ending (Progrès fixed; Musette and WoO 82 open).
- Accidentals are spelled by key signature direction only (flats in flat keys, sharps in sharp keys). I fixed the clear case (raised notes in flat keys, 20 notes). About 30 cases in sharp keys (e.g. Chopin Waltz, The Entertainer, March of the Wooden Soldiers: A# where B flat is meant) are still spelled with the wrong accidental; the sound is the same. The converter (outside catalogue/) should spell by melodic direction.
- Tempos in arrangement.txt are deliberately slower than the Mutopia MIDI (for learners).

## Legal concerns

1. Bella Ciao: origin disputed (see above); EU term to 2037 if Ziganoff were the composer. Keep unverified until P2-18.
2. No composer or lyricist died after 1955 in any piece (latest: Mildred J. Hill 1916, Joplin 1917, Lyte 1913). All 27 Mutopia pages say "Public Domain"; all printed sources are before 1930 (Chopin Waltz: Peters 1900; Joplin: 1899 and 1902 originals).
3. Largo: to check one rhythm (D E equal eighths in bar 7) I looked at the Mutopia typesetting of the symphony, which is CC BY-SA. I used it only to read the rhythm and copied nothing; the arrangement stays CC0 and own work. If you prefer, re-check bar 7 against an old Simrock print.
4. Traditional flag: no piece is a 'traditional' tune with a living-composer trap. Mary Had a Little Lamb was corrected the other way (it was credited to Mason although the common tune is not his). Row Row Row Your Boat is credited to Lyte (died 1913) in an 1881 book, but Wikipedia says it is unclear whether he composed or adapted it; public domain either way. Happy Birthday: melody by Mildred Hill (1916), words disputed between the Hill sisters (Patty Hill died 1946); no words are in the score; fine in the EU.
5. Bach Musette BWV Anh. 126 is not securely by Bach; harmless legally.
